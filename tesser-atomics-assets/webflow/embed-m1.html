This is the fuel: a graphite ball about the size of a golf ball.

Inside, thousands of TRISO particles sit in a band just under the surface.

Each particle is smaller than a poppy seed.

And each one wraps its uranium kernel in layers of carbon and silicon carbide, a containment that outlasts the radioactivity.

Proven fuel that contains itself.

It's called TRISO. Two decades of U.S. Department of Energy investment went into developing and testing it, and reactor makers across the industry now use it. Every pebble holds thousands of tiny specks of uranium, each sealed inside its own layered shell.
